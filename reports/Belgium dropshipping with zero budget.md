# Sell AI films, not dropshipped gadgets

Do not start a dropshipping store with €0 in Belgium in 2026. On 2025-2026 benchmarks, a typical €30 product shipped from China leaves about €10 per sale for advertising, while the median Meta ad cost per purchase is about $38, and you would have to pay your supplier and the ad platform before payment providers release your customers' money. Since 1 July 2026, each China-to-consumer parcel also pays a €3 EU duty per item category, a further €2-per-item handling fee is expected from 1 November 2026, and EU law makes you, not your Chinese supplier, answerable for product safety, refunds and a two-year guarantee. The better path is the one your own portfolio already points to: sell directed AI video (product films and batches of ad variations) and cinematic websites to Belgian and Benelux businesses, where pricing guides put directed AI ads at roughly $150-$600 each, small businesses typically budget €2,000-€5,000 for a good website, and client deposits can pay for your tools before you spend anything. Legally, you need a €111.50 business registration once a client is ready to buy, a social insurance fund, and a VAT number under the small-business exemption that you can activate yourself online; social contributions then cost between €0 and about €460 a quarter, depending on whether you are a student, an employee doing this on the side, or self-employed full-time. A realistic target is first revenue within weeks and €1,000-€3,000 a month within three to six months, while a million a year would mean about 33 clients each paying €2,500 every month: an agency with a team, built over years. Claude can do much of the building, writing and drafting, but it cannot find your clients, register your business or send the roughly 300 messages the first month needs. Check the legal, tax and fee figures on the official Belgian sites listed in the final section before you act.

## Each sale earns about €10 but costs about $38 to win

A dropshipping store is a webshop that passes each order to a supplier, usually in China, who ships straight to your customer, so you never hold stock. Opening one is nearly free. Shopify's current offer, as reported for the US, is three free days and then $1 a month for three months ([eSEOspace](https://eseospace.com/blog/shopify-free-trial/); [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/small-business/shopify-pricing)). After that, the Basic plan costs roughly **€21-€32 a month** in Belgium, depending on billing and source ([Flatline Agency](https://www.flatlineagency.com/blog/shopify-payments-vs-third-party-psps); [EcomCalcHub](https://ecomcalchub.com/shopify-fee-calculator-be)). Supplier platforms such as CJ Dropshipping are free to join ([Super-DS](https://super-ds.com/blog/cjdropshipping-review-2026)).

What you cannot get free is customers. Two advertising terms decide the outcome. The **cost per acquisition** (CPA) is the ad spend needed to win one sale. The **conversion rate** is the share of visitors who buy. CPA equals the cost per click divided by the conversion rate.

The researchers' worked example takes a €29.95 impulse product (€24.75 after VAT) and assumes €9 for the item and its shipping from China. It then subtracts three costs: Shopify's Belgian card fee of 2% + €0.25 ([Clickforest](https://www.clickforest.com/en/blog/webshop-development-cost)), the new €3 EU duty, and a 5% allowance for refunds. That leaves about **€10.40 per sale** for advertising before you make or lose a cent, and the expected €2 handling fee would cut it to about €8.40. Here is what a sale actually costs to win:

| How the ad cost per sale is estimated | Ad cost per sale |
|---|---|
| Meta (Facebook and Instagram) median cost per purchase across e-commerce brands, 2025 ([Triple Whale](https://triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks)) | **$38.17** |
| Meta median clicks of $0.57-$0.82 at the average Shopify conversion rate of 1.4% ([Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/?p=45854); [Vaizle](https://insights.vaizle.com/facebook-ad-benchmarks/); [Nudgify](https://www.nudgify.com/shopify-conversion-rate-benchmarks/)) | $41-$59 |
| The same clicks at 3.2%, the rate that puts a store in Shopify's top 20% | $18-$26 |
| TikTok clicks in Europe at €0.30-€0.80, converting at 1.4% ([Fullmetrix](https://fullmetrix.com/en/blog/tiktok-ads-ecommerce-guide)) | €21-€57 |

Every line is above the €10 you can afford. These are international benchmarks dominated by established brands; no Belgian figures were found. A higher price does not close the gap: at €49.95 with a €15 product cost, the same example leaves €19-€20, still below Meta's median. The numbers only work for stores in the top tenth for conversion, stores whose bundles raise the order value, or stores with repeat buyers. A new store has no reviews and an ad account that has not yet learned who buys, so it is more likely to sit below the 1.4% average than above it.

Even finding out costs money. Practitioner rules drop a product once it has spent 1.5-2 times its break-even CPA across three to five ad versions without a sale ([AdAdvisor](https://adadvisor.ai/blog/facebook-ads-for-dropshipping); [DoDropshipping](https://dodropshipping.com/tools/dropshipping-test-budget-calculator/)). By the researchers' estimate, that puts a single test at €50-€200 and the search for one product that works at several hundred euros. One new store owner reported spending about $150 on Meta and TikTok ads for 60-70 visits and no sales ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/zero-sales/354665)).

The second problem is timing. You pay the supplier when an order arrives and the ad platform as you spend, but your customers' money can be held back. Shopify's own help pages describe **reserves** (a share of your payouts withheld), such as 10% of every sale held for 120 days ([Shopify Help Center](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/reserves)). One merchant was told that 20% of income would be held for 120 days "due to the nature of my business model (dropshipping)" ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/20-hold-on-all-income-from-shopify-please-help/66487)). PayPal holds new sellers' payments for up to 21 days ([PayPal](https://www.paypal.com/au/webapps/mpp/funds-availability)).

Slow delivery adds to the risk. Standard shipping from China takes 10-15 working days ([Logistik-Journal](https://www.logistik-journal.de/aliexpress-lieferzeit/)), which invites "item not received" chargebacks: card disputes that the customer's bank reverses. Visa reportedly cut the dispute rate it tolerates in Europe to 1.5% in April 2026 ([Chargeflow](https://www.chargeflow.io/blog/vamp-visa-acquirer-monitoring-program)). New advertisers also report Meta ad accounts disabled on their first day ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/facebook-banned-ad-account-on-my-first-day-need-help-to-appeal/64475)). With €0, the only way to cover the gap is a credit card or a loan. microStart, for example, lends €500-€25,000 to people without bank access at roughly 9-12% interest ([VLAIO](https://www.vlaio.be/nl/subsidies-financiering/subsidiedatabank/microkredieten-van-microstart)). That is how failed tests turn €0 into debt.

The third problem is that you would be reselling what Belgians can already buy more cheaply. Temu reported **115.7 million monthly users in the EU** in the first half of 2025 ([Retail Insight Network](https://www.retail-insight-network.com/news/beuc-temu-complaint-europe/)), and TikTok Shop opened in Belgium on 15 June 2026 ([RetailDetail](https://www.retaildetail.eu/news/general/tiktok-shop-to-launch-in-belgium-and-the-netherlands-in-june/)). Belgians spent a record **€18.3 billion online in 2025**, yet Belgian webshops grew only 3.43%, because most of the growth went to foreign platforms ([Belga News Agency](https://www.belganewsagency.eu/belgian-online-spending-tops-18-billion-euros-but-foreign-retailers-take-a-growing-share); [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/06/04/belg-koopt-steeds-vaker-online-maar-inkomsten-vloeien-vooral-na/)).

Shoppers are also being taught to spot resellers. Test-Aankoop, Belgium's main consumer organisation, tells them to check whether a webshop's products are sold elsewhere for a fraction of the price. It also warns that a .be address or Dutch-language text does not prove a seller is local ([Test-Aankoop](https://www.test-aankoop.be/familie-prive/webshops/nieuws/dropshipping)). The federal economy ministry, FOD Economie, received more than 5,000 reports about fraudulent webshops in 2025 ([VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2025/12/07/30-000-klachten-consumentenproblemen/)).

Nobody can even tell you your odds. The often-repeated claim that 90% of stores fail traces back to no study at all ([Dropship.io](https://www.dropship.io/blog/dropshipping-statistics)). The success stories you see online come from the few who succeeded or who sell courses.

## Since July 2026, the legal risk sits with you, not your supplier

Dropshipping is legal in Belgium. FOD Economie says so, while warning that many such shops mislead buyers or hide who runs them ([Forum for the Future](https://blog.forumforthefuture.be/nl/article/dropshipping-riskante-online-koopjes-extra-voorzichtigheid-is-geen-luxe-/29303)). But a store does not let you skip registering a business. Mollie, a common Belgian payment provider, asks for an enterprise number and a VAT number before it will process payments ([Mollie](https://help.mollie.com/hc/en-us/articles/30937649332498-What-additional-information-does-Mollie-need-to-open-a-Mollie-Business-Account)). TikTok Shop wants a **KBO number** (your entry in the Kruispuntbank van Ondernemingen, Belgium's business register, called BCE in French), a VAT number and a business bank account ([Clickforest](https://www.clickforest.com/en/blog/tiktok-shop-belgium)). Belgium also has a low-tax "sharing economy" regime for small side incomes, but it covers services between private people through approved platforms, not reselling goods ([Unizo](https://www.unizo.be/advies/actief-binnen-de-deeleconomie-hoeveel-belastingen-betalen-wat-met-sociale-bijdragen)). Every registration cost in the plan below therefore applies to a store as well. A store then adds several layers of obligations that a service business mostly avoids.

**Customs and VAT.** Since **1 July 2026**, parcels worth up to €150 sent from outside the EU to consumers pay a **€3 customs duty for each item category** inside them, until 1 July 2028. For example, a parcel holding two toys, a coat and three bottles of shampoo pays €9 ([Council of the EU](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/02/11/council-gives-final-green-light-to-new-customs-duty-rules-for-small-parcels/)), whatever VAT arrangement the seller uses ([European Commission](https://trade.ec.europa.eu/access-to-markets/en/news/eu-applies-eu3-customs-duty-item-low-value-e-commerce-consignments)). On 21 September 2026, the European Commission also adopted a **€2-per-item handling fee** on goods from outside the EU at any value. The fee is not refunded when goods are returned. It is expected from **1 November 2026** unless the European Parliament or Council objects, and it had not been confirmed in force when this research was done ([VitalLaw](https://www.vitallaw.com/news/eu-confirms-new-e-commerce-handling-fee-will-be-eur2-per-item/gdn01174716); [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/09/23/europa-stelt-afhandelingstaks-voor-worden-kleine-pakjes-van-bui/)).

Belgium's **small-business VAT exemption** (the *vrijstellingsregeling*, or *franchise* in French) lets a business with under €25,000 of annual turnover charge no VAT. If you use it, though, the import VAT you pay on Chinese goods becomes a cost you can never reclaim ([ClearTax](https://www.cleartax.com/be/nl/vat-exemption-scheme-belgium); [Loyens & Loeff](https://www.loyensloeff.com/nl/l-l_e-commerce-nl.pdf)). By the researchers' arithmetic, a €20 item picks up about **€9.83 in duty, handling fee and import VAT, almost half its value**.

Two EU schemes come into play as you grow. **IOSS** (the Import One-Stop Shop) lets a seller charge the buyer's VAT at checkout on imported parcels worth up to €150 and declare it monthly ([SBB](https://www.sbb.be/nl/node/2123)); how it combines with the Belgian exemption is unclear. **OSS** (the One-Stop Shop) applies once your sales to consumers in other EU countries pass €10,000 a year. From then on, you charge each customer their own country's VAT, and you can declare it all through one Belgian return ([practicali](https://www.practicali.be/blog/intracommunautaire-afstandsverkopen-en-de-oss-aangifte)).

**Product safety.** Since 13 December 2024, the EU's General Product Safety Regulation (**GPSR**) has banned selling a consumer product unless a business based in the EU is responsible for it. Every online listing must show the manufacturer, that EU responsible person, a product identifier, and safety warnings in the customer's language ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/NL/legal-content/summary/general-product-safety-regulation-2023.html); [Vimm](https://www.vimm.be/en/blog/gpsr-article-19-product-information-webshop)). Dutch government guidance says a dropshipper selling non-EU goods can be treated as the importer, or as the manufacturer if it puts its own brand on them ([business.gov.nl](https://business.gov.nl/products-services-and-innovation/product-safety/product-safety-for-importers/)). An importer must check the **CE marking** (the manufacturer's declaration that a product meets EU safety law) and its paperwork. It must also put its own name and address on the product or packaging ([Compliance Gate](https://www.compliancegate.com/ce-marking-importers/)) and keep records for at least 10 years ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/bel/legal-updates/the-new-eu-general-product-safety-regulation-what-you-need-to-know)).

That matters because cheap goods often fail these rules. When Test-Aankoop and partner groups tested 162 products sold through Temu and Shein, **112 (70%) broke EU rules**, including 53 of 54 toys and 52 of 54 USB chargers ([Test-Aankoop](https://www.test-aankoop.be/familie-prive/webshops/pers/het-gevaar-van-shein-en-temu-bevestigd)). As a sole trader (*eenmanszaak*), you answer for such failures with your personal assets.

Packaging, electrical goods and batteries also bring in Belgian "extended producer responsibility" schemes, under which sellers help pay for recycling: Fost Plus for packaging, Recupel for electrical goods, and Bebat for batteries. The old 300 kg packaging threshold, below which small sellers were exempt, reportedly stopped applying when the EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR) took effect on 12 August 2026 ([WKO](https://www.wko.at/oe/abfall/ppwr-belgien); [Bebat](https://www.bebat.be/en/blog/permits-webshop)).

**Consumer law.** Belgian buyers get **14 days to withdraw** from an online purchase, extended by 12 months if you did not inform them properly ([Test-Aankoop](https://www.test-aankoop.be/familie-prive/webshops/nieuws/terugsturen-van-onlineaankoop)). The refund must include standard delivery ([Captain Legal](https://www.captain.legal/nl/dagelijkse-zaken/herroepingsbrief-online-aankoop-model-in-word-en-pdf/)). On top of that, a **two-year legal guarantee** rests on you, not the factory ([Unizo](https://www.unizo.be/ondernemersgids/bedrijfsvoering/marketing-en-verkoop/garantie-0)).

Since 14 August 2026, webshops must also offer an online withdrawal button. The duty comes from a law of 19 July 2026 that added article VI.61/2 to Belgium's Code of Economic Law (WER), although one law firm dates it from 19 June ([droitbelge.be](https://droitbelge.be/news_detail.asp?id=1199); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/bel/legal-updates/belgian-consumer-law-reform-new-rules-on-online-sales-subscription-renewals-sustainability-claims-and-consumer-complaints)). Your identity, KBO number, VAT number, address, phone number and email must appear on every page ([Securex](https://www.securex.be/nl/blog/ondernemers/uw-e-shop-wettelijk-in-orde-in-7-stappen)). These rules are enforced: inspectors found violations at **67.3% of the 2,664 businesses** they checked in 2024 ([Forum for the Future](https://blog.forumforthefuture.be/nl/article/aandachtpunt-7-op-10-gecontroleerde-bedrijven-in-overtreding-met-economische-wetgeving/28168)).

Platforms and campaigners are also pushing back against the China-direct model. bol, the best-known marketplace in Flanders, does not allow sellers to ship to its customers from outside the EU ([bol](https://partnerplatform.bol.com/en/idp/dropshipping)). The Dutch consumer association Consumentenbond studied 20 dropshippers that had drawn more than 2,200 complaints on Trustpilot, and has called for misleading dropshipping to be banned ([Consumentenbond](https://www.consumentenbond.nl/nieuws/2025/20-dubieuze-dropshippers-ontmaskerd-aanpak-hard-nodig); [Twinkle](https://twinklemagazine.nl/2026/08/consumentenbond-wil-verbod-op-misleidende-dropshipping/index.xml)).

### If you still want to sell products, sell them this way

Selling physical products is not the mistake; generic China-direct dropshipping paid for with ads is. A version with a realistic chance would start only once your service income can fund it, and it would reverse each weakness above.

It would source inside the EU, which avoids the €3 duty and the €2 fee and means delivery in days. EU-warehouse suppliers advertise 2-10 days, but they need a subscription (BigBuy reportedly starts at about €69 a month) or stock you buy upfront ([ZIK Analytics](https://www.zikanalytics.com/blog/european-dropshipping-suppliers/); [Spocket](https://www.spocket.co/blogs/cjdropshipping-overview)). Print-on-demand with EU production is the other option: each item is made only after a customer orders, and Gelato's own comparison lists an EU-made T-shirt at $6.58 ([Gelato](https://www.gelato.com/ca/gelato-vs-printful)).

It would list only products whose suppliers name an EU responsible person, and avoid toys, electronics, batteries and cosmetics. It would also sell only within Belgium at first, because the packaging regulation can require an authorised representative in every other EU country you ship to ([Dreyfus](https://www.dreyfus.fr/en/2026/08/20/ppwr-since-12-august-2026-packaging-epr-compliance-has-become-a-prerequisite-for-online-sales/)).

It would sell where buyers already are instead of paying for traffic. TikTok Shop Belgium charges about 9% commission ([TokMargin](https://tokmargin.com/en/markets/be)). Amazon.com.be charges €0.99 per item on its Individual plan, plus category fees ([Amazon](https://sell.amazon.com.be/en/tarifs)). bol accepts goods shipped from inside the EU. At checkout it would offer Bancontact, Belgium's domestic debit-card payment method, which about 73% of Belgian online shoppers use ([Tembi](https://www.tembi.io/blog/payment-providers-in-european-e-commerce-a-country-by-country-analysis); [PayRequest](https://payrequest.io/guides/bancontact-payment-guide)) and for which Stripe charges a flat €0.35 ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/en-be/pricing/local-payment-methods)). It would ship through bpost pick-up points, from €5.40 a parcel ([bpost](https://www.bpost.be/sites/default/files/pricing/2026/Praktische_gids_voor_particuliere_zendingen_2026_NL.pdf)).

Above all, it would sell something tied to an audience you can reach for free, such as prints or merchandise from your own films once people follow them. If you want an e-commerce experiment that costs nothing now, affiliate programmes pay you a commission for referring buyers, with no stock at all; bol pays 4-8% on the sales you refer ([bol](https://affiliate.bol.com/nl/handleiding/commissiemodel-affiliate-programma/)). The strongest idea close to products, though, is to sell to e-commerce sellers instead of becoming one.

## Directed AI video pays $150-$600 an ad, and the client pays first

A service business reverses the cash problem. A client pays a deposit before you spend anything, and there is no stock, ad budget or refund reserve to finance. Demand is rising. In a 2025 survey by the IAB, the US advertising industry body, **86% of video-ad buyers** were using or planning to use generative AI to make video ads. They expected AI to be behind 40% of video ads by 2026, and smaller brands were adopting it faster than large ones ([PR Newswire/IAB](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/nearly-90-of-advertisers-will-use-gen-ai-to-build-video-ads-according-to-iabs-2025-video-ad-spend--strategy-full-report-302504691.html)). Malt, a European freelance marketplace, reported that companies' demand for AI projects grew 230% between 2023 and 2024, while the number of freelancers with AI skills grew only 31%. Most of that demand is for technical work rather than video ([ITdaily](https://itdaily.com/news/business/ai-skills-wanted-in-europe-malt)). The IAB data is mostly American, and no Belgian survey of small businesses' demand for AI video was found.

Prices split into two markets, and only one is worth your time. Commodity "AI UGC" clips (synthetic videos styled as customer testimonials) sell for $5-$100 on Fiverr ([Fiverr](https://www.fiverr.com/gigs/dropshipping-videos); [Genra](https://genra.ai/blog/ai-video-freelancer-business-guide)), and self-serve AI tools now make them for $3-$25 each ([UGC Copilot](https://ugccopilot.ai/blog/ai-ugc-video-creator-software-vs-freelancer-2026/); [Sparkiz](https://sparkiz.ai/blog/ugc-ads-cost/)). Directed work covers the concept, the script, the hooks (the opening seconds that stop someone scrolling), brand consistency, editing and responsibility for results. 2026 pricing guides put it at roughly **$150-$600 per finished ad, $1,200-$4,000 for a batch of 8-15, and $2,500-$8,000 a month for a retainer**, which is a fixed monthly fee for ongoing work ([Motionbox](https://motionbox.io/blog/how-much-to-charge-clients-for-ai-ugc-ads); [Channel.farm](https://channel.farm/blog/how-to-price-ai-video-services-freelancer-agency)).

The local alternatives you would undercut cost more. Belgian motion designers on Malt list €350-€480 a day ([Malt](https://fr.malt.be/s/tags/motion-design-59ca868d6e6c295df40aa43c)). A Dutch cost guide puts a testimonial video at €1,000-€4,000 and a brand film of two to five minutes at €3,500-€15,000 ([Searchlab](https://searchlab.nl/kosten/wat-kost-een-bedrijfsvideo-laten-maken)). A Belgian price guide puts a typical small-business budget for a quality website at **€2,000-€5,000**, with freelancers billing €30-€80 an hour and maintenance adding €200-€1,000 a year ([LUNIDEV](https://lunidev.com/nl/blog/website-kosten-2026-complete-prijsgids-belgie)). Most of these figures come from vendors and single listings, so treat them as ranges to test rather than settled facts. Your portfolio already puts you on the directed side of this split, with the tagline "I direct films that were never shot", AI-generated footage and a scroll-driven 3D section. It also shows you can build the kind of site those budgets buy.

Your interest in dropshipping helps here, because you understand online sellers' biggest cost, which is ads. So sell them the ads. Skip dropshippers themselves: they buy ad videos on Fiverr for $5-$75 a gig ([Fiverr](https://www.fiverr.com/gigs/dropshipping-videos)), and Fiverr keeps 20% of each order ([Genra](https://genra.ai/blog/ai-video-freelancer-business-guide)). Aim instead at established Benelux brands that advertise all the time, and offer them batches of hook variations rather than "a video". Making versions of an ad for different audiences is the top use ad buyers report for generative AI ([PR Newswire/IAB](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/nearly-90-of-advertisers-will-use-gen-ai-to-build-video-ads-according-to-iabs-2025-video-ad-spend--strategy-full-report-302504691.html)).

Your second offer is for local businesses such as restaurants, hotels, estate agents, clinics and event organisers: a cinematic website with a 20-30 second hero film and short versions cut for social media. The researchers estimate this could sell for €2,500-€5,000, which fits within the budgets above. Write to Flemish prospects in Dutch and Walloon prospects in French. Belgians prefer to buy in their own language, and even their choice of shopping platform splits along the language line ([Landmark Global](https://landmarkglobal.com/uploads/Factsheet_2024_Belgium_EN_v03.pdf)). A .be domain helps with local buyers: the registry's own surveys, now dated, found .be the most trusted web address ending in Belgium ([DNS Belgium](https://www.dnsbelgium.be/en/news/market-survey-online-security)).

Three risks shape how you sell. The first is that audiences distrust obvious AI. In a Harris Poll, **73% said they would trust an ad less if they suspected AI had made it**, and 63% would be less likely to buy ([Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/harris-poll-ai-fatigue-less-trust-ai-generated-ads-cannes-lions)). The research firm NIQ found that such ads can leave a "negative halo" on the brand ([Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/consumer-perceptions-generative-ai-in-marketing-openai-sora/735761/)). So blend real product footage with AI scenes, and never fake customer testimonials.

The second risk is legal. Since **2 August 2026**, Article 50 of the EU AI Act has required anyone using AI professionally to label realistic AI-generated images, audio or video that resemble real people, places or events, which the law calls deepfakes. The label must be visible from the first moment ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-publishes-code-practice-marking-and-labelling-ai-generated-content); [Peterka Partners](https://blog.peterkapartners.com/article-50-of-the-ai-act-in-practice-transparency-of-ai-systems-content-labeling-and-deepfakes/)). Clearly artistic or fictional work can use a lighter, credits-style disclosure. However, commentators argue that ordinary ads do not qualify for that exception, and they cite fines of up to €15 million or 3% of turnover ([European Commission FAQ](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act); [Legalithm](https://www.legalithm.com/en/blog/ai-act-transparency-obligations-article-50-deepfake-labeling); [Fontvera](https://fontvera.eu/intelligence/ai-act-faq-deepfakes)). Copyright is the other legal issue: purely AI-generated output is unlikely to be protected in the EU ([European Parliament Research Service](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/BRIE/2025/782585/EPRS_BRI%282025%29782585_EN.pdf)). Your contracts should therefore transfer rights "to the extent they exist" rather than promise exclusive ownership.

The third risk is that tools change under you. Dreamina is the app through which ByteDance offers Seedance, and its free tier bars commercial use for users outside the US ([AdTestBench](https://adtestbench.com/tools/seedance/pricing/)). Kling's free tier reportedly does the same ([CloudZero](https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/kling-ai-pricing/)). Sora's app closed in April 2026 and its API in September ([The Post Flow](https://thepostflow.com/ai/where-sora-users-go-now/)). Build the business on your direction, not on one model. Entry-level paid plans cost about $10-$20 a month each: Kling Standard is $10, Dreamina is $15 after a $0.29 first month, and Runway Standard is $15 ([CloudZero](https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/kling-ai-pricing/); [Dreamina](https://dreamina.capcut.com/seedance/seedance-2-0-pricing); [Techpresso](https://academy.techpresso.co/reviews/is-runway-worth-it)). One client deposit covers that.

The other zero-capital models the researchers compared are weak as a main income. One estimate found that half of the Notion templates with visible sales on Gumroad earned under $520 in their lifetime ([InsightRaider](https://insightraider.com/en/answers/how-much-can-you-make-selling-notion-templates-on-gumroad)). AI-made designs already make up 12-15% of new Etsy and Redbubble listings ([AIBusiness.vc](https://aibusiness.vc/solo/ai-art-print-on-demand)). YouTube tightened its monetisation rules against mass-produced AI content in July 2025 ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/2025/07/10/youtube-is-fighting-ai-slop-with-new-monetization-guidelines/)). These models work better as marketing for the service business; a behind-the-scenes channel, for example, doubles as a portfolio that brings in leads.

### What a million would actually take

The pitch of an AI-built store that earns millions has a history. In September 2024, the US Federal Trade Commission sued Ecommerce Empire Builders. It alleged that the company sold dropshipping stores "powered by artificial intelligence" with claims that buyers could earn $10,000 a month "and could even make millions". A court halted the business, and in May 2025 the defendants agreed to be banned from selling business opportunities ([FTC](https://search.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/empire-holdings-group-llc-et-al-ftc-v); [FTC complaint](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2423027ecommercebuilderscomplaint.pdf)). Treat any promise of that kind as marketing.

The service path has no verified millionaires either. The researchers found no independently checked case of a solo AI-video creative earning €5,000-€20,000 a month. The cases they did find are self-reported, such as a two-person AI video agency said to make $20,000 a month ([AI Video Bootcamp](https://aivideobootcamp.com/blog/making-10k-month-ai-video/)).

At small scale, the arithmetic is still encouraging. Two retainers at €2,500 make €5,000 a month. The researchers' realistic planning range for months three to six is **€1,000-€3,000 a month** from one to three small clients. A million a year, by contrast, is about €83,000 a month. That means roughly 33 retainers at €2,500, or about ten at $8,000, the top of the cited range, whereas the researchers estimate that one person using AI tools can serve three to six retainer clients. A million a year is therefore an agency with a team, built over years: a real destination, but not a starting plan.

Festival prizes are the nearest thing to a jackpot. Runway's AI Film Festival awards a $50,000 Grand Prix, and the Astana AI Film Festival lists a $450,000 Grand Prize ([Runway AIF](https://aif.runwayml.com/); [AAIFF](https://aaiff.ai/)). Treat them as a source of credibility with a lottery attached.

## A 90-day plan that needs little more than €111.50 up front

The plan follows one rule: spend time before money, and spend money only after a client has paid. For the first two to three weeks, you build proof and start conversations at no cost. You register the business when a prospect asks for a quote, and you buy a paid video plan out of the first deposit. From day 60, you turn one-off projects into monthly work.

The outreach targets rest on published benchmarks. Cold emails average a **4.5% reply rate** ([Hunter](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email)); 1-4% is the most common result, and personalised messages get two to three times the engagement ([Mailshake](https://assets.mailshake.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/16091740/Cold-Email-Report-2025-Mailshake.pdf)). About 28-30% of LinkedIn connection requests are accepted, and adding a short note lifts replies from 5.44% to 9.36% ([Expandi](https://expandi.io/?p=14040)). Messages sent after connecting get replies about 10% of the time ([JoinValley](https://www.joinvalley.co/blog/why-linkedin-outreach-ignored-reply-rate-benchmarks)). By the researchers' arithmetic, 300 personalised emails plus 300 LinkedIn requests should lead to roughly 17-23 conversations. If 10-20% of those convert, that means two to five first clients after three to four weeks of daily effort. A spec piece is an unpaid sample made for one specific prospect. No documented case shows one winning a contract, so treat that tactic as a reasoned bet based on the personalisation data, not a proven route.

### The legal minimum, in the order to do it

Your status decides whether Belgium's fixed costs in year one are €111.50 or about €2,000, so settle it first. VLAIO (Flanders' business-support agency), hub.brussels (on 1819) and the 1890 line in Wallonia all give free first advice ([hub.brussels](https://info.hub.brussels/en/node/92); [VLAIO](https://www.vlaio.be/nl/subsidies-financiering/subsidiedatabank/starten-vanuit-werkloosheid-steunmogelijkheden)). Ask them, too, at what point preparing and contacting prospects counts as starting the activity, because the research did not settle where that line falls.

If you are an employee working at least half-time (235 hours a quarter), you can be self-employed **in bijberoep**, meaning as a side activity ([VLAIO](https://www.vlaio.be/nl/begeleiding-advies/start/ondernemen-bijberoep/voorwaarden-om-te-starten-bijberoep)). You then pay no social contributions on 2026 profits below **€1,922.16**. Above that, contributions are 20.5% of taxable income, with a minimum of €98.51 a quarter ([Accountable](https://www.accountable.eu/nl-be/blog/sociale-bijdragen)). If you are a student aged 18-25 and enrolled for at least 27 credits, the **student-zelfstandige** (student-entrepreneur) status exempts you below **€8,687.04** of net taxable income, but only if you request the exemption ([VLAIO](https://www.vlaio.be/nl/begeleiding-advies/start/student-zelfstandige/veelgestelde-vragen/veelgestelde-vragen-na-de-opstart-van-je-student-onderneming); [Securex](https://www.securex.be/nl/inspiratie/ondernemerschap/starten/een-voordelig-dubbelleven-als-student-zelfstandige-5-weetjes)).

If you receive unemployment benefits, the **springplank naar zelfstandige** (springboard to self-employment) from the national employment office, RVA/ONEM, lets you keep benefits for up to 12 months while you test a side activity. You must declare the activity on form C1C before you start ([VLAIO](https://www.vlaio.be/nl/subsidies-financiering/subsidiedatabank/starten-vanuit-werkloosheid-steunmogelijkheden)). Bear in mind that unemployment benefits are being limited to two years from 2026 ([RIZIV/INAMI](https://www.inami.fgov.be/nl/thema-s/arbeidsongeschiktheid/werknemers-en-werklozen/beperking-van-werkloosheid-in-de-tijd-impact-op-uw-recht-op-arbeidsongeschiktheids-of-moederschapsuitkeringen)).

If none of these fits, the law treats you as self-employed in your main occupation ([VLAIO](https://www.vlaio.be/nl/begeleiding-advies/start/werkloosheid-en-ondernemen/je-hebt-een-zelfstandig-bijberoep-en-wordt)). As a first-timer, called a **primostarter**, you owe at least **€459.82 a quarter** whatever you earn, compared with a regular minimum of €890.42 for those who are not first-timers ([RSVZ](https://socialsecurity.belgium.be/sites/default/files/content/docs/nl/sociaal-beleid-vorm-geven/zelfstandigen/bijdragennota_2026_tabel_nl.pdf)). With the registration fee and fund charges, that comes to about €2,000 in year one. If you live in Wallonia and start full-time, ask Le Forem, the regional employment service, about the Airbag grant of up to €12,500 over two years. It requires approved training or support, and you must apply before joining a social insurance fund or at the latest within a month after ([Le Forem](https://www.leforem.be/entreprises/aides-financieres-creation-activite-independant-airbag.html)).

|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | If you receive unemployment benefits, declare the activity on form C1C. If you are in Wallonia and starting full-time, check the Airbag deadline. | €0 | Before step 3 |

Watch out for one timing trap. The €25,000 VAT-exemption limit is cut in proportion to the part of your first year that remains when you start. A business starting on 1 July gets about €12,603 ([Forum for the Future](https://blog.forumforthefuture.be/nl/article/wat-houdt-de-btw-vrijstellingsregeling-voor-kleine-ondernemingen-in/30644)). By the same day count, starting on 1 November 2026 leaves only about €4,200 of VAT-free turnover for 2026, and starting on 1 December leaves about €2,100. A single €2,500 website-and-film bundle invoiced in December could therefore push you out of the scheme.

If you go over the limit by up to 10%, you stay exempt until 31 December and then move to the normal VAT regime. If you go over by more, you switch at once to charging VAT and filing regular VAT returns, and you cannot rejoin the scheme until 1 January of the second year after ([Accountable](https://www.accountable.eu/nl-be/blog/wijzigingen-2025-btw-vrijstelling-kleine-ondernemingen/)). If your first deals are large, ask the business counter or an accountant whether to start on 1 January 2027 or how to time your invoices.

A few standing rules complete the picture. You do not need a company. A BV/SRL (a private limited company) requires a notary's deed and a financial plan ([Partena](https://www.partena-professional.be/nl/starten-als-zelfstandige/statuut-en-rechtsvorm/besloten-vennootschap-bv-oprichten)), whereas a sole proprietorship is enough. No region requires a business-management diploma any more, since Wallonia dropped it on 1 October 2025 ([Securex](https://www.securex.be/nl/lex4you/zelfstandige/nieuws/basiskennis-bedrijfsbeheer-verdwijnt-als-startvoorwaarde-in-waalse-gewest)).

Profits are taxed as professional income in progressive bands from 25% to 50% ([Accountable](https://www.accountable.eu/nl-be/blog/progressieve-belastingschijven/)), after a tax-free allowance of about €10,910 for 2025 income ([BILLY](https://billy.tech/nl/de-belastingschijven-en-de-belastingvrije-som-in-belgie/)). If you are employed, side profits are added to your salary and taxed at your highest rate, which can reach 50% ([VLAIO](https://www.vlaio.be/nl/begeleiding-advies/start/zelfstandige-bijberoep/je-rechten-en-plichten-als-zelfstandige-bijberoep); [Test-Aankoop](https://www.test-aankoop.be/geld/belastingen/nieuws/zelfstandige-in-bijberoep-belastingen)).

Sole traders with turnover under €500,000 can keep simple books in three journals: one for cash and bank, one for purchases and one for sales ([VLAIO](https://www.vlaio.be/nl/begeleiding-advies/start/verplichtingen-na-de-start/voer-een-heldere-boekhouding)). You must keep supporting documents for at least 7 years ([Accountable](https://www.accountable.eu/nl-be/blog/boekhouding-eenmanszaak/)). Since 1 January 2026, invoices between Belgian businesses must be structured e-invoices sent over **Peppol**, an EU network for machine-readable invoices, and businesses under the VAT exemption must at least be able to receive them ([Unizo](https://www.unizo.be/ondernemersgids/bedrijfsvoering/digitalisering/e-facturatie-correct-werken-met-peppol-en-je); [Accountable](https://www.accountable.eu/nl-be/blog/e-facturatie/)). If your site uses analytics or advertising cookies, visitors need a real choice to accept or refuse them. Belgium's data-protection authority has fined one cookie breach €15,000 ([Computable](https://www.computable.be/?p=10394)).

## Claude can replace a small agency team, not a salesperson

An AI assistant changes the economics of a service business far more than those of a store. In dropshipping, the costs that decide the outcome are ad prices, payout holds, duties and compliance, and Claude cannot lower any of them. The store it could build for you in an afternoon is one every competitor can build too. In a one-person studio, Claude can stand in for the developer, copywriter, translator and contract drafter you could not otherwise afford, which is what makes a €0 start realistic. What it cannot supply is the scarce part: clients, trust and a legal identity.

## Conclusion

The verdict on dropshipping gives you a test to reuse on every idea that comes after it. With no capital, start only things where the customer's money arrives before yours leaves. And sell only what Temu, a Fiverr gig or the next AI model cannot copy cheaply. Generic dropshipping fails both halves of the test, which is why no amount of AI help rescues it. A directed AI film studio passes both, which is why the same help becomes leverage. The cheaper AI makes stores, sites and clips to produce, the more the value moves to taste, judgment and trusted relationships with buyers. That is the part of "films that were never shot" that no prompt reproduces.

The million question also looks different under that test. A store is a single bet that ads will pay for themselves. A studio is a ladder. Each rung, whether the first invoice, the first retainer or the first editor you subcontract, proves itself within weeks, and each can pay for the next, riskier step. That next step could one day include products sold to an audience your films have built. Nobody can know today whether you will reach a million. Whether a Belgian business will pay you for a film is something you can find out within 60 days, for €111.50.

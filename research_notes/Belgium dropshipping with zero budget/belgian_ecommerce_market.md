# Belgian (and Benelux) E-commerce Market 2025-2026, as Seen by a New Zero-Budget Belgian Seller

*Method note: research date 2026-10-24. Every web page fetch (WebFetch, curl) failed in this environment, either with DNS errors or because the proxy refused the connection. All findings below therefore come from search-engine result extracts of the cited pages, not from reading the full pages. Treat exact figures, especially fees, as "verify on the official page before relying on them". Where extracts conflicted, this is flagged.*

## 1. Market size, growth, foreign share and top categories (2024-2026)

### Takeaway
Belgians spent about EUR 18.3bn online in 2025, up 5.4% (Becom Market Monitor, published around June 2026). Belgian webshops grew only 3.43%, so most of the growth went to foreign players: Dutch marketplaces (bol, Coolblue), Amazon, and especially Shein and Temu, which are growing 20-30% a year. By the measures available, roughly one-third of online spending and about three-quarters of online parcels go to or come from foreign sellers. Clothing and footwear is the largest goods category.

### Inferences
- Belgium is a large, mature but slow-growing domestic market. A tiny new seller competes for a share of about EUR 18bn, where the growth is captured by platforms rather than independent Belgian webshops.
- Clothing, electronics and beauty are the biggest categories, but they are exactly where Shein, Temu, Zalando, bol and Coolblue dominate on price and range. Generic dropshipped fashion or gadgets put a newcomer head-to-head with the cheapest players.
- The headline "foreign share" depends on the measure: about a third of spending, about three-quarters of parcels, and over half of shoppers. The report should not quote a single figure without naming its measure.

## 2. Consumer preferences and trust: payments, delivery, trust labels, attitudes to Chinese and dropshipping shops

### Takeaway
Payments: Bancontact is the must-have payment method (about 70-73% of online shoppers use it), followed by cards and PayPal. Payconiq is being replaced by Wero and Bancontact through 2026, and Wero went live for Belgian e-commerce in March 2026.

Delivery: Belgians still prefer free home delivery. Pick-up points are cheaper to ship to (bpost from EUR 5.40) and widely accepted.

Trust and Chinese platforms: Belgian consumers are increasingly wary of fake and dropshipping shops. FOD Economie receives thousands of fraudulent-webshop reports a year, and in late 2025 Test-Aankoop found 70% of the Temu and Shein products it tested non-compliant. The EU removed the EUR 150 duty exemption and has charged EUR 3 per parcel since 1 July 2026. All of this weakens the "Chinese dropshipping webshop" model specifically.

### Inferences
- Bancontact plus cards is the minimum viable checkout, with PayPal a useful third option. Wero is becoming relevant through 2026 and costs nothing extra to enable via Mollie or Stripe-type providers. A shop without Bancontact will look foreign.
- A new seller's cheapest credible delivery offer is bpost to a pick-up point, at about EUR 5.40 per parcel under 2 kg, in 1-2 days. Belgians nevertheless prefer free home delivery, so the shipping cost has to be built into product prices.
- Fake-shop warnings from the FOD, VRT and Test-Aankoop, together with the Temu/Shein safety findings, mean an unknown Belgian webshop starts with a trust deficit. A generic product range, long delivery times, and no company details or Belgian address make it look exactly like the shops consumers are warned about.
- The 2026 customs changes add EUR 3 per item (and possibly EUR 2 more) to each China-to-consumer parcel. This erodes the price advantage that makes dropshipping from China viable for tiny sellers.

## 3. Language: Dutch, French, German, English

### Takeaway
Belgian consumers clearly prefer to shop in their own language, and platform loyalty splits along language lines: bol in Flanders, Amazon (and French players) in Wallonia. A Belgian shop is therefore expected to work in at least Dutch and French. No quantified conversion-uplift study for Belgium was found.

### Cited Findings
- Landmark Global's 2024 Belgium factsheet advises presenting a webshop in both Dutch and French because Belgians prefer shopping in their own language. It also says Dutch-speaking Belgians prefer Dutch platforms and French-speaking Belgians prefer French platforms — [Landmark Global factsheet 2024](https://landmarkglobal.com/uploads/Factsheet_2024_Belgium_EN_v03.pdf)

### Inferences
- Dutch plus French is effectively mandatory for a Belgium-wide own webshop. English is a low-value extra, useful mainly for Brussels expats. German serves a very small market.
- A one-person seller can stage the work: launch in the language they are fluent in (one region) and add the second language once products prove themselves.
- Marketplaces partly solve the language problem. bol is Dutch-first and strong in Flanders but also serves French-speaking Belgian customers; Amazon is strongest in Wallonia.

## 4. Marketplace alternatives: requirements, costs and fit for a seller with EUR 0

### Takeaway
Every serious marketplace requires a registered business:
- bol, Amazon (professional), TikTok Shop and Vinted Pro all want a KBO/BCE enterprise number, usually a VAT number, ID, and a business bank account.
- Registration is mostly free. Costs are per sale: bol about 5-17% commission plus a fixed fee per item, Amazon a EUR 0.99 per-item or EUR 39 per-month plan plus referral fees, and TikTok Shop about 9%.

bol explicitly bans shipping to customers from outside the EU, which rules out China-direct dropshipping. Its delivery-promise metrics punish slow delivery.

The genuinely zero-cash entry routes are:
- 2dehands/2ememain (free listings and a free seller page)
- Vinted (free to sell)
- Etsy, for handmade or vintage only (about USD 0.20 per listing)
- bol's affiliate programme (no stock at all)

  - Selling one's own used items on 2dehands/2ememain or Vinted
  - Becoming a bol (or Amazon) affiliate
- Opening a sole proprietorship (eenmanszaak), possibly as a side job, is the gateway to bol, Amazon, TikTok Shop, Vinted Pro and Mollie/Stripe. The KBO number itself can be obtained quickly online (see Section 5); social-security contributions as self-employed are outside this scope.
- Suitability for a tiny Belgian seller:
  - **bol:** the best reach in Flanders, but China-direct dropshipping is forbidden. Late deliveries hurt seller metrics, and fees take roughly 15-20% once the fixed fee is included. It works best with EU-sourced or locally held stock.
  - **Amazon.com.be:** strongest in Wallonia. The EUR 0.99-per-item Individual plan avoids a fixed monthly cost.
  - **TikTok Shop:** new in Belgium (June 2026), so there is first-mover potential and organic reach without an existing audience. It needs a registered business and content creation.
  - **2dehands:** free and local, good for testing demand.
  - **Etsy:** only fits handmade, vintage or craft products.
- Marketplaces solve the "no audience" problem, because buyers are already there, but they cut margins. An own .be webshop keeps margin but must create traffic from zero. With no ad budget that means SEO and social content, which takes months.

## 5. Payment providers for Belgian webshops: costs and onboarding

### Takeaway
All mainstream payment providers (Mollie, Stripe, Shopify Payments) onboard businesses, not private individuals, and require a KBO enterprise number and ID verification, usually also a business bank account. A Belgian sole trader below EUR 25,000 turnover can use the VAT exemption scheme, but still has to register with the VAT administration.

Bancontact costs about EUR 0.25-0.39 per transaction:
- Stripe Belgium: a flat EUR 0.35
- Mollie: either a flat EUR 0.39 or 1.40% + EUR 0.25 (sources conflict)

Shopify charges a EUR 21-32 monthly plan fee, which is a fixed cost a zero-budget seller may want to avoid at first.

### Cited Findings
- **Stripe Belgium:** Bancontact costs EUR 0.35 per successful charge, plus 2% if currency conversion is needed (official local payment methods page). Third-party calculators quote 1.4-1.5% + EUR 0.25 instead — [Stripe BE – local payment methods pricing](https://stripe.com/en-be/pricing/local-payment-methods); [Stripe BE pricing](https://stripe.com/en-be/pricing); [PayRequest](https://payrequest.io/guides/bancontact-payment-guide)

  - Asks for the company registration number, VAT number and an ID copy of the legal representative(s)
  - Verification takes about 5 working days, and the 1-cent verification payment takes 2-3 days
  - An older source (for a non-profit) says only organisations with an enterprise number can apply, and personal bank accounts are not allowed

- **Shopify in Belgium (conflicting):**
  - Basic plan at EUR 21 per month billed yearly or EUR 28 billed monthly (Sept 2026), with a 2% extra fee when using a third-party payment provider on Basic — [Flatline Agency](https://www.flatlineagency.com/blog/shopify-payments-vs-third-party-psps)
  - A Belgium fee calculator lists EUR 32 per month and a Shopify Payments card rate of 2.0% + EUR 0.25 — [EcomCalcHub](https://ecomcalchub.com/shopify-fee-calculator-be)

  - Applies at turnover up to EUR 25,000 excl. VAT, for any legal form and also for side-job self-employed

### Inferences
- With no fixed monthly fees, Stripe and Mollie keep the cash cost of a webshop's checkout near zero until sales happen. Shopify adds about EUR 21-32 a month, plus 2% on top when using Mollie for Bancontact.
- Bancontact's flat per-transaction fee (about EUR 0.25-0.39) is far cheaper than card percentages on typical EUR 20-50 baskets. This favours making Bancontact the default checkout option.
- The VAT exemption lets a starter avoid charging VAT. Platforms such as bol and the payment providers may still expect a VAT number, which exempt businesses also receive.

- PayPal Business fees in Belgium, and Shopify Payments' Bancontact rate, were not found.
- Self-employed social-security contributions (Belgium) for a side-job webshop were not researched; they are relevant to the "zero budget" framing.

## 6. Value of an existing .be domain

### Takeaway
.be is Belgium's dominant domain extension, with about 1.70 million active names at the end of 2025. Registry-commissioned surveys say Belgians trust .be sites most, and that the share of people who avoid untrustworthy-looking sites is rising (73% in 2020). An existing .be domain is therefore a modest but real local trust signal. It does not compensate for missing trust basics: HTTPS, company details, a known payment method and reviews.

### Cited Findings
- **DNS Belgium annual report 2025 / press release:**
  - 1,701,999 active .be names at the end of 2025, a decrease of 16,117 (-0.94%), mainly because one large foreign platform removed inactive names
  - 207,776 new .be registrations in 2025, about 7,500 more than in 2024
  - .be renewals fell by about 1.00%
  - The top 3 registrars hold 36.33% of names
  - Sources: [DNS Belgium press release](https://www.dnsbelgium.be/en/news/belgian-internet-continues-grow-nearly-208000-new-be-domain-names-2025); [DNS Belgium Annual Report 2025](https://www.dnsbelgium.be/en/annual-report-2025)
- **DNS Belgium market surveys (registry-commissioned, so self-interested):**
  - Late 2018: 64% of Belgians do not visit websites that seem unreliable
  - This share went from 52% in 2019 to 73% in 2020
  - .be was the most trusted extension (2019); .be and .com inspired most trust (2020)
  - HTTPS and signs of an "official" site are the other main trust factors
  - Sources: [DNS Belgium – market survey online security](https://www.dnsbelgium.be/en/news/market-survey-online-security); [DNS Belgium market survey 2020](https://www.dnsbelgium.be/en/accelerated-digitalisation); [Telecompaper](https://www.telecompaper.com/news/belgians-more-aware-of-online-security-be-sites-most-trusted--1281659)
- DNS Belgium says 75% of entrepreneurial Belgians choose .be over other extensions — [DNS Belgium – why .be](https://www.dnsbelgium.be/en/news/why-be-best-choice-belgian-entrepreneurs)
- DNS Belgium actively revokes malicious .be sites and increasingly blocks them before they go live (20% fewer revocations needed in 2025) — [DNS Belgium press release](https://www.dnsbelgium.be/en/news/belgian-internet-continues-grow-nearly-208000-new-be-domain-names-2025)

### Inferences
- An existing .be domain saves money (registration is already paid) and signals "Belgian business". This matters because many Chinese dropshipping shops use generic .com or .shop names and appear in FOD warnings.
- A .be is not a safety guarantee in consumers' eyes. Fake shops have used .be, which is why the registry screens for them. So the domain must be paired with visible trust markers: a KBO/VAT number on the site (legally required anyway), a Belgian address and phone, Bancontact, clear returns terms, and later the Becom Trustmark.
- Being registry-run surveys from 2018-2020, the trust data is dated and self-interested.

### Gaps
- No independent or recent (2024-2026) study was found quantifying whether a .be domain raises webshop conversion.
- The number of .be domains running webshops was not found.

## 7. Local advantages a Belgian seller has over Chinese platforms

### Takeaway
A Belgian micro-seller cannot beat Temu or Shein on price. It can beat them on the things Belgian consumers say they worry about:
- Speed: next-day bpost delivery or a pick-up point, against long China transit times now taxed at EUR 3 or more per parcel
- Safety and EU compliance, at a time when 70% of tested Temu and Shein items failed EU rules
- Native Dutch/French service and an easy return within Belgium
- Visible, verifiable Belgian business identity
- Local, niche, handmade or B2B products

### Cited Findings
- 62% of Belgian users of Chinese platforms have experienced problems with purchases (Becom survey, 2026) — [RetailDetail NL](https://www.retaildetail.nl/news/algemeen/chinese-platformen-en-ai-dubbele-bedreiging-voor-belgische-webshops/)
- 70% of tested Temu and Shein products were non-compliant (Test-Aankoop, Oct 2025) — [Test-Aankoop](https://www.test-aankoop.be/familie-prive/webshops/pers/het-gevaar-van-shein-en-temu-bevestigd)
- 3 in 4 consumers would not visit a physical Temu or Shein store (Comeos/UNIZO poll) — [Unizo](https://www.unizo.be/berichten/pers/acht-op-de-tien-belgen-plannen-shoppinguitstap-tijdens-weekend-van-de-klant)
- An EUR 3 EU duty per low-value parcel since 1 July 2026, with an additional EUR 2 handling fee proposed — [Test-Aankoop](https://www.test-aankoop.be/familie-prive/consumentenrechten/nieuws/belasting-kleine-pakketten-aankoop-buiten-de-eu); [VRT NWS Sept 2026](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/09/23/europa-stelt-afhandelingstaks-voor-worden-kleine-pakjes-van-bui/)
- Domestic shipping costs EUR 5.40 to a bpost pick-up point for parcels up to 2 kg (2026) — [bpost 2026 guide](https://www.bpost.be/sites/default/files/pricing/2026/Praktische_gids_voor_particuliere_zendingen_2026_NL.pdf)
- Becom positions Temu and Shein as "unfair competition" that often sells non-compliant goods, and lobbies for stronger government measures — [Ecommercenews.nl](https://www.ecommercenews.nl/belgische-ecommerce-18-miljard-euro-waard-in-2025/); [Gondola: "Becom neemt het op tegen Shein en Temu"](https://www.gondola.be/nl/news/becom-neemt-het-op-tegen-shein-en-temu-amazon-treedt-hun-voetsporen)
- Structural headwinds for Belgian sellers compared with Dutch competitors: higher Belgian VAT, some stricter national rules, and faster logistics from the Netherlands (Comeos) — [Gondola](https://www.gondola.be/nl/news/comeos-wil-gelijke-regels-heel-de-benelux)
- The Becom Trustmark also certifies B2B webshops — [Becom](https://becom.digital/nl/installatie-becom-trustmark/)
- Amazon.com.be shows that Belgian SMEs can sell at scale through a marketplace with local-language support: more than 10 million products sold by 2024 — [RetailDetail EU](https://www.retaildetail.eu/news/general/belgian-smes-sell-20-products-a-minute-on-amazon/)
- Electrical products sold via marketplaces in Belgium must comply with Recupel take-back obligations (since 29 March 2025). This is a compliance burden that also filters out non-compliant foreign sellers — [o1.eu](https://o1.eu/news-insights/how-to-sell-on-tiktok-shop-belgium-2026-operator-guide)

### Inferences
- The defensible niche for a zero-budget Belgian seller is the opposite of generic China dropshipping:
  - Products that are local, regional, artisanal, refurbished/second-hand or curated
  - Products that are compliance-sensitive (toys, chargers, cosmetics, jewellery), where EU-compliant stock is a selling point
  - Fast delivery, a native-language "real person" service, and an easy return to a Belgian address
- If dropshipping is used at all, EU-based suppliers (Belgium, Netherlands, Germany) shipping in 1-3 days fit both bol's rules and consumer expectations. China-direct shipping fits neither, and the 2026 duties make it more expensive.
- B2B selling to Belgian SMEs, local shops and associations (custom-printed goods, office or horeca supplies) is a low-competition avenue Chinese consumer platforms do not serve well. It is reachable through direct outreach at zero cost. This inference is not quantified by any source found.
- A realistic zero-budget path:
  1. Validate products on free channels: 2dehands/2ememain, Vinted, TikTok organic content.
  2. Register a sole proprietorship (side job) with the VAT exemption.
  3. Add bol and/or Amazon.com.be for reach, plus TikTok Shop for content-driven discovery.
  4. Use the existing .be webshop with Mollie or Stripe (Bancontact first) as the trust anchor and margin-keeping channel for repeat customers.

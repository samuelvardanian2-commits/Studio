# Dropshipping economics and success rates in 2026: Belgian seller starting with €0

Research method note: in this session, direct page fetching was blocked by the network proxy (WebFetch and curl both failed). All findings below come from web-search result extracts of the cited pages, not from full-page reads. Numbers should be spot-checked against the cited URL before they are quoted as precise. Most "industry" numbers on dropshipping come from vendor/affiliate blogs that sell tools, courses or supplier services. They are marked as such. Dates are flagged where data is older than 2025.

## 1. What fraction of new dropshipping stores fail, and how fast?

### Takeaway
No credible primary study measures dropshipping store failure. The "80-95% fail" and "90% fail within 120 days" figures repeated everywhere cannot be traced to any dataset, and Shopify does not publish merchant failure or churn data. The best hard proxy is general small-business survival (US BLS: about 20.6% of all new businesses close in year 1). Dropshipping almost certainly does worse than that because the barrier to entry is so low, but the size of the gap is not measured. The public "success" evidence is heavily skewed by survivorship bias and by course sellers. Several sellers of this kind have been sued by the US FTC for false earnings claims.

### Cited Findings
- Dropship.io (a dropshipping tool vendor) states in its 2026 statistics page that it found no traceable global study behind the widely repeated "90% failure" claim, and concludes there is no credible global dropshipping success rate or universal average profit margin. — [Dropship.io, Dropshipping Statistics 2026](https://www.dropship.io/blog/dropshipping-statistics)
- Blog roundups repeat 80-90% (some 95%) first-year failure as "industry estimates" without naming a study. Other roundups claim 10-20% "consistent profitability" and 1-5% "long-term success", again with no named sample or definition. — [TrueProfit](https://trueprofit.io/blog/dropshipping-success-rate); [Dropship Lifestyle (course seller)](https://www.dropshiplifestyle.com/dropshipping-success-rate-statistics/); [Ecommerce Paradise](https://ecommerceparadise.com/how-many-dropshippers-fail-the-real-statistics-and-how-to-beat-the-odds/); [Super-DS](https://super-ds.com/blog/dropshipping-statistics-2026)
- Shopify does not publish an official merchant failure rate. The "90% fail within 120 days" claim for Shopify stores is described even by blogs that repeat it as "largely unverified" and not traceable to a primary study. Estimates that 5-10% of Shopify stores reach long-term profitability are also unsourced. — [GroPulse](https://gropulse.com/shopify-success-rate/); [HasThemes](https://hasthemes.com/blog/shopify-success-rate/); [NAS.com](https://nas.com/blog/why-shopify-stores-fail)
- One vendor blog claims only 10% of new Shopify stores remain active after 90 days. This could not be verified anywhere else. — [search-result summary of Shopify-failure roundups; e.g. Upsella "70% churn" page](https://upsella.com/blog/statistics/shopify-churn-rate-2026) (treat as unverified)
- Closest government proxy (all US businesses, not e-commerce specific): about 20.6% close within the first year, and only 34.7% survive a full decade (US Bureau of Labor Statistics, as cited). — [TermsAndConditionsTemplate citing BLS](https://termsandconditionstemplate.com/what-percentage-of-shopify-stores-fail)
- The only concrete platform dataset surfaced is a US TikTok Shop analysis in which more than half of stores recorded no sales, while a small group exceeded seven figures in GMV. The exact publisher could not be confirmed from the search extract, which came from 2026 success-rate roundups. — [TrueProfit roundup](https://trueprofit.io/blog/dropshipping-success-rate) (attribution uncertain)
- Survivorship bias and guru claims, hard evidence: in September 2024 the US FTC sued Ecommerce Empire Builders (Empire Holdings Group, Peter Prusinowski). It alleged the defendants sold dropshipping stores "powered by artificial intelligence" with claims that buyers could earn $10,000/month "and could even make millions". Programs cost almost $2,000 and "done for you" stores cost tens of thousands. A court halted the operation and appointed a receiver. In May 2025 the defendants agreed to an order banning them from selling business opportunities. — [FTC complaint PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2423027ecommercebuilderscomplaint.pdf); [FTC case page](https://search.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/empire-holdings-group-llc-et-al-ftc-v); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/e-commerce-dropshipping-course-seller-faces-ftc-deception-suit)
- Older (2023) parallel case: the FTC's Automators AI case concerned e-commerce "business opportunity" and coaching programs that allegedly caused over $22 million in consumer harm. The defendants presented themselves as self-made e-commerce millionaires. A separate FTC action (Lunar Capital Ventures / Ecom Genie / Profitable Automation) concerned an operation that took more than $12 million with false promises of returns from Amazon/Walmart selling. — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/86373); [FTC complaint](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/1-Complaint.pdf)
- A trade blog reports a July 2025 FTC permanent ban against another e-commerce business-opportunity operation, with combined judgments of $118.5M of which about $13M was expected to be paid. This is not verified against an FTC press release. — [Ecommerce Paradise](https://ecommerceparadise.com/breaking-ftc-iyovia-judgment-2026-05-23-midday/)
- The FTC's consumer-protection director warned consumers to be cautious about money-making opportunities promising significant earnings, "especially those spread on social media". — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/going-business?page=1)

### Inferences
- The honest answer for the report is that the failure rate is unknown, but the weight of indirect evidence points to "most stores never become profitable". The evidence: no-sales rates in platform data, zero-sales anecdotes, and the unit economics in section 3. Quoting "90%" as a fact would repeat guru marketing.
- Survivorship bias is structural. The people who publish revenue screenshots are the minority who succeeded or who sell courses. Revenue screenshots also hide ad spend, refunds and COGS. The FTC cases show that the "proof" content in this niche is sometimes outright deceptive.
- Failure in dropshipping is usually fast and quiet. Stores are abandoned after the trial or the first failed ad tests rather than formally closed, so no registry of closed businesses captures them. This is one reason why no good data exists.

### Gaps
- No EU or Belgium-specific survival data for webshops or dropshipping stores was found. Statbel or Graydon data on young e-commerce company survival was not searched or found.
- No data from Shopify, Store Leads or BuiltWith on the share of new stores that never record a sale or that close within 3-12 months was found.
- Dropship.io's "data from 20 stores" could not be read because fetching was blocked, so its sample-level numbers are missing.

## 2. Unavoidable launch and test costs in 2026 (platform, apps, domain, samples, ad test budget, CPM/CPC/CPA)

### Takeaway
The store itself can be launched almost free. Shopify's 2026 offer is 3 days free, then $1/month for 3 months, then about $39/month (about €33 in euro markets) on Basic. The unavoidable cost is traffic. With 2025-2026 Meta median CPA around $38 (Triple Whale) and European CPMs of roughly $10-12 (FR/DE), a meaningful test of even one product needs on the order of €100-300 of ad spend. A realistic search for one working product usually takes several tests. Belgium also adds roughly €189 of one-off registration costs (KBO plus VAT activation via an ondernemingsloket).

It is valid only for first-time Shopify users, and Shopify cannot extend trials or reset promo pricing. Stores created via an AI-tool or "vibe code" partner reportedly get a 4-month trial instead. — [eSEOspace](https://eseospace.com/blog/shopify-free-trial/); [VPNCentral](https://vpncentral.com/?p=254353); [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/small-business/shopify-pricing)
- Basic is $39/month billed monthly. Annual billing gives 25% off Basic, Grow and Advanced. One source lists $290/year, which is inconsistent with "25% off $39" (about $351/year), so verify. — [WebsiteBuilderExpert](https://www.websitebuilderexpert.com/ecommerce-website-builders/shopify/shopify-pricing-pp/); [SheroCommerce](https://sherocommerce.com/shopify-pricing/)

**Test budget per product**
- No universal test budget exists. Practitioner and vendor heuristics: compute breakeven CPA from your own unit economics, use 3-5 creatives per test, and review or kill at 1.5-2x breakeven CPA with no purchase. Kill thresholds of 1x/1.5x/2x target CPA are used with minimum conversion counts before scaling. Fixed rules such as "kill after $20" are misleading. If you can only fund one or two proper tests, spreading money across six products tells you little. — [AdAdvisor](https://adadvisor.ai/blog/facebook-ads-for-dropshipping); [DoDropshipping test-budget calculator](https://dodropshipping.com/tools/dropshipping-test-budget-calculator/); [WinningHunter](https://winninghunter.com/blog/facebook-ads-for-dropshipping)
- Anecdotal: a new store spent about $150 on Meta and TikTok and got 60-70 visits and zero sales. Another got about 3.5k sessions from TikTok ads and zero sales. — [Shopify Community "Zero Sales"](https://community.shopify.com/t/zero-sales/354665); [Shopify Community "Lots of sessions but no sales"](https://community.shopify.com/t/lots-of-sessions-but-no-sales/370230)

### Inferences
- Illustrative minimum cash need for a Belgian first attempt. It uses the cited prices plus my assumptions, so it is not a sourced total. The fixed costs are:
  - €0-3 for Shopify during the promo, then about €33/month
  - about €189 one-off for KBO plus VAT activation
  - a domain and a product sample, which are unsourced and modest
  
  Ad tests come on top of that. At breakeven CPAs of €10-20 (see section 3), a 1.5-2x kill rule and 3-5 creatives, one product test costs about €50-200 before you have a verdict. Realistic budgets for finding one product that works are therefore several hundred euros or more. This lines up with the zero-sales anecdotes at the $150 level, where 60-70 visits is far too little data to judge anything.
- Belgium/Netherlands escape the new Meta location surcharge (per secondary sources), a small relative advantage vs France/Austria from July 2026.
- The real launch cost is risk capital for ads, not software. Software is nearly free for the first 3 months.

### Gaps
- No Belgium- or Netherlands-specific Meta or TikTok CPM, CPC or CPA figures were found. Statista has a "Facebook ads CPM by country Feb 2025" chart, but the values were not retrievable.
- No 2025-2026 Google Ads or Shopping CPC benchmark for Benelux e-commerce was found.
- No sourced 2026 pricing was gathered for WooCommerce (free plugin, but hosting is extra), Wix, Shopify apps or domain registration (.be/.com). These are generally small (single to low double-digit €/month), but no source was retrieved in this session.
- Product sample costs (typically product plus China shipping) were not sourced.
- Belgian social-security contributions for a side business (bijberoep) were not researched. They can be a recurring cost once income exceeds thresholds.

## 3. Typical gross and net margins after all costs; conversion rates for new stores

### Takeaway
Vendor blogs claim 10-20% net margins for "well-run" stores, with ad spend at 20-40% of revenue (one claimed sample averages 36%, range 18-62%). These numbers come from survivors, and none has verifiable methodology. Run the cited median ad costs (Meta CPA about $38; CPC $0.57-0.82 at a 1.4% average Shopify conversion rate) against a typical €25-50 impulse product. The median outcome is loss-making unless the store reaches top-quintile conversion rates or much higher order values.

### Cited Findings
- Claimed net margin: 15-20% for experienced sellers (Branvas) and 10-25% for "well-run" stores (Earnifyhub). Both are vendor or affiliate sites. — [Branvas](https://branvas.com/blogs/news/is-dropshipping-profitable); [Earnifyhub](https://earnifyhub.com/learning-guides/dropshipping-profit-margins-real-numbers-2026)
- Claimed cost structure as % of revenue:
  - COGS 30-45% (USAdrop)
  - ad spend 20-35% (USAdrop), or an average of 36% in a claimed 30-store sample, ranging 18-62% (Earnifyhub)
  - refunds and chargebacks 2-5% (USAdrop)
  - return rates 5-8% of orders and chargeback fees of $15-25 per incident (Earnifyhub)
  - platform and payment fees 3-6%
  
  None of the samples' methodology is verifiable. — [USAdrop](https://usadrop.com/dropshipping-profit-margin-real-numbers-improve/); [Earnifyhub](https://earnifyhub.com/learning-guides/dropshipping-profit-margins-real-numbers-2026)
- Shopify conversion rate benchmarks (Littledata, 2024 report as cited by secondary sites):
  - average 1.4% across Shopify stores
  - above 3.2% puts a store in the top 20%, and 4.7% or more in the top 10%
  - add-to-cart rate 4.6% and checkout completion about 45%
  - mobile about 1.2%, desktop about 1.8-1.9%
  
  Broader e-commerce benchmarks are higher (Dynamic Yield global 2.66%), but they are not comparable. — [Nudgify](https://www.nudgify.com/shopify-conversion-rate-benchmarks/); [Blend Commerce](https://blendcommerce.com/en-gb/blogs/shopify/ecommerce-conversion-rate-benchmarks-2025)
- Meta median CPA $38.17 (Triple Whale 2025, DTC brands that are mostly established, not new stores). — [Triple Whale](https://triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks)
- Breakeven CPA method example (US): price $49.99 − product $21 − shipping/fees $3.75 = $25.24 available for ads and profit. The guide suggests a target CPA of about $17. — [AdAdvisor](https://adadvisor.ai/blog/facebook-ads-for-dropshipping)
- Belgian fees: card 2% + €0.25 on Shopify Basic. — [Clickforest](https://www.clickforest.com/en/blog/webshop-development-cost)
- From 1 July 2026, the EU charges €3 customs duty on low-value parcels (≤€150) from outside the EU. Most sources say it is charged per item category (6-digit tariff code) within a consignment, and one says per item. Sellers, importers or their representatives pay in most cases. It runs until July 2028, when normal duties are expected to apply. A separate €2 per-parcel handling fee has been proposed, with no date. — [Retail Gazette](https://www.retailgazette.co.uk/blog/2026/07/shein-and-temu-hit-by-new-eu-customs-duty-on-cheap-parcels/); [Cyprus Mail](https://cyprus-mail.com/2025/12/12/eu-to-impose-e3-duty-on-e-commerce-parcels-from-july-2026); [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/tech/eu-taxes-low-value-parcels-aliexpress-shein-temu/)

### Inferences
Worked example. It uses my own assumptions for product cost and is meant to show the structure of the economics, not to give precise values.

**Inputs**

| Item | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Price | €29.95 incl. 21% VAT, so €24.75 net | Assumption |
| Product plus China shipping | €9 | Assumption |
| Shopify card fee | €0.85 | Cited 2% + €0.25 |
| EU duty (one item category) | €3 | Cited |
| Refund/chargeback allowance | €1.50 (5%) | Cited range |

**Result:** contribution before ads is about €10.40, so breakeven CPA is about €10.

**Ad cost per purchase against that breakeven**

| Scenario | Cost per purchase |
|---|---|
| Median Meta CPC $0.57-0.82 at 1.4% conversion | about $41-59 per purchase (CPC ÷ CVR) |
| Same CPC at top-20% conversion (3.2%) | about $18-26 |
| TikTok Europe CPC €0.30-0.80 at 1.4% | about €21-57 |
| Triple Whale Meta median CPA | $38 |

Every one of these is above the €10 breakeven.

**Higher price point (€49.95):** with about €15 product cost, the contribution is about €19-20. That is still below the median CPA. It is only viable with top-decile conversion, upsells or bundles that lift order value, or repeat purchases.

**What this means**
- For a newcomer (below-average conversion, untrained ad-platform pixel), the expected first-months result is negative contribution. Claimed 10-20% net margins describe surviving, optimised stores, which is survivorship bias.
- Under Belgium's VAT exemption (turnover below €25k) the seller keeps the gross price, but cannot reclaim input VAT. That improves the per-unit maths somewhat for very small volumes. Cross-border EU B2C sales may trigger destination VAT, which is not verified in this session.
- New-store conversion is very likely below the 1.4% average, because there are no reviews, no brand recognition and a cold pixel. This is inference only; no new-store cohort data was found.

### Gaps
- No verifiable P&L dataset (audited or platform-level) for dropshipping stores was found. All margin figures are vendor illustrations.
- No conversion-rate data specific to new stores or to the first 90 days was found.
- No EU-specific return-rate data for dropshipped goods was found. EU 14-day withdrawal rights likely make returns higher and costlier than US figures, but this is unquantified.
- The VAT treatment of a Belgian exempt small business selling cross-border to NL/FR consumers (the €10k EU distance-selling threshold and OSS) was not verified in this session.

## 4. Payment processor risk: reserves, payout delays, freezes, and the €0 cash-flow trap

### Takeaway
Officially, Shopify Payments can impose fixed reserves (e.g. $1,000 for 120 days) or percentage reserves (e.g. 10% of each sale for 120 days). PayPal holds new sellers' funds for up to 21 days. Visa's merchant dispute threshold tightened to 1.5% in the EU from April 2026. Dropshippers report extra scrutiny, such as 20% held for 120 days after a sales spike. With €0, the seller must pay the supplier and the ad platform before customer money is released. That makes a credit card or cash float effectively mandatory.

### Cited Findings
- Shopify's official help page: reserves are either fixed-amount (example: $1,000 set aside for 120 days) or percentage-based (example: 10% of each transaction held for 120 days, with the remainder paid normally). — [Shopify Help Center, Reserves](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/reserves)
- Regular Shopify Payments pay periods are 3 days in Germany and 3 calendar days in France (non-customisable, €10 minimum payout), plus 1-3 business days for the bank. Blogs claim the first payout for a new account can take up to 7 days because of verification, which is not confirmed on official pages. — [Shopify Help, Germany](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/supported-countries/germany/accepting-payments); [Shopify Help, France](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/supported-countries/france/pay-periods); [Nudgify](https://www.nudgify.com/when-does-shopify-payout/)
- Anecdotal, from the Shopify Community:
  - one merchant was told that "due to the nature of my business model (dropshipping)" Shopify would hold 20% of income for 120 days because of chargeback risk after a sales spike
  - other threads describe 120-day holds, payouts held for 4 weeks, and new-account holds with no payout dates
  - one responder says Shopify sometimes keeps funds pending until delivery is confirmed for brand-new stores
  
  — [Shopify Community, 20% hold](https://community.shopify.com/t/20-hold-on-all-income-from-shopify-please-help/66487); [120-day hold](https://community.shopify.com/t/why-is-shopify-holding-my-funds-for-120-days/246036/12); [4 weeks held](https://community.shopify.com/t/shopify-payouts-held-for-4-weeks-no-clear-resolution/644134); [New account payout hold](https://community.shopify.com/t/new-account-payout-hold-shopify-payments-verified-orders-received-but-payout-status-still-pending-with-no-dates/646493); [New store payouts on hold](https://community.shopify.com/t/new-store-payouts-on-hold/379565)
- PayPal (official): new sellers may not be able to withdraw immediately. Payments show as pending and are released after 21 days if all goes well. To graduate from holds you need at least 25 transactions, $250 in sales and 90 days since your first sale. PayPal also uses rolling reserves (a percentage of every transaction held and released on schedule). — [PayPal funds availability (AU)](https://www.paypal.com/au/webapps/mpp/funds-availability); [PayPal US](https://www.paypal.com/us/webapps/mpp/security/ebay-payments-learn-more)
- A vendor (Fena, UK, Sept 2025) says rolling reserves of 10-30% of revenue for 90-180 days are triggered by new accounts, high-risk categories, order spikes and elevated disputes. It says dropshippers face "more frequent and more aggressive monitoring". — [Fena](https://www.fena.co/blog/why-paypal-freezes-funds-for-uk-shopify-merchants-—-what-triggers-it-and-how-to-respond)
- Practical mitigations reported by merchants: upload tracking for every order, keep proof of fulfilment, and keep a backup processor. — [Shopify Community, PayPal holds](https://community.shopify.com/t/experiences-with-paypal-holding-funds-as-a-new-seller/16264)
- Visa Acquirer Monitoring Program (VAMP):
  - the "excessive" merchant threshold dropped from 2.2% to 1.5% on 1 April 2026 for the EU, US, Canada, APAC and LATAM
  - enforcement began 1 October 2025
  - fraud disputes are effectively double-counted, so a merchant at 0.8% under the old method can be at 1.5% under VAMP
  - fees are reported at about $8 per dispute, with reserves and termination risk
  
  All of this comes from vendor summaries, not Visa documents. — [Chargeflow](https://www.chargeflow.io/blog/vamp-visa-acquirer-monitoring-program); [Fraud.net](https://fraud.net/resources/visas-vamp-thresholds-drop-to-1-5-on-april-1-are-you-ready); [Chargeback Gurus](https://www.chargebackgurus.com/blog/visa-announces-even-more-vamp-changes)
- Chargeback fees are $15-25 per incident (vendor). — [Earnifyhub](https://earnifyhub.com/learning-guides/dropshipping-profit-margins-real-numbers-2026)

### Inferences
- The €0 cash-flow trap is a timing problem, made worse by China shipping times:

| Day | Customer money | Seller's costs |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | Customer pays; money goes to Shopify | Seller pays the supplier immediately (CJ/AliExpress charge at order) |
| Days 0-7 | Not yet paid out | Ad platform bills spend at thresholds or daily |
| Day 3-7+ | Payout arrives only if there is no new-account hold or reserve | |
| Up to 2-3 weeks | Some new stores report funds held until delivery confirmation; delivery is 10-15 working days from China | |
| 120 days | Any reserve (10-20%) is locked | |

- As a result, each sale requires the seller to front product cost and ad cost. Scaling up makes this worse: a good week multiplies the float needed.
- Someone with literally €0 needs either a credit card, which shifts risk to personal debt, or a cash float. A realistic float is at least one to two weeks of ad spend plus supplier costs. Running ads on a credit card is how inexperienced sellers end up with debt when tests fail.
- Chargeback exposure is structurally higher for China-shipped dropshipping. Long delivery times plus "item not as described" claims lead to disputes, and the tightened 1.5% VAMP threshold leaves little room. At small volumes, a handful of disputes can push the ratio over.

### Gaps
- No official Stripe reserve documentation was retrieved (Stripe is relevant for WooCommerce stores).
- No official Shopify statement on first-payout timing or on delivery-confirmation holds for Belgium was found. The Belgium pay-period page was not retrieved; the DE/FR pages are used as proxies.
- No data was found on Meta/TikTok billing thresholds for new EU advertiser accounts.

## 9. What works better in 2026, and the "dropshipping is dead / not dead" debate

### Takeaway
Practitioner and vendor consensus, all of it commercially interested, is that *generic* AliExpress/"winning product" dropshipping is dead or dying. Ad costs are rising (CPM up about 16-20% in 2025), Temu and TikTok Shop compete on the same products, the EU and US have closed duty loopholes, and BE/NL consumer groups are campaigning against misleading webshops. What survives is closer to normal retail: branded niche stores, EU-based or own-label supply, POD with real design or audience value, and marketplace or social-commerce selling. All of these need either capital, time or a distinctive asset (audience, design, expertise).

### Cited Findings
- Vendor and blog consensus: generic stores selling identical AliExpress products face intense competition. Businesses that solve specific customer problems, source quality products and provide good service "continue to thrive". Winning stores look like established retail brands. — [Dropship Lifestyle](https://www.dropshiplifestyle.com/is-dropshipping-dead/); [LetsTalkShop](https://www.letstalkshop.com/blog/is-dropshipping-dead-in-2026); [Spocket (supplier, interested)](https://www.spocket.co/blogs/is-dropshipping-dead-in-2026)
- SourcingXpro (a China sourcing agent, interested party) says experienced operators stopped chasing "winning products" years ago, and that speed alone is no advantage because Temu competes on price and delivery. — [SourcingXpro](https://sourcingxpro.com/why-dropshipping-is-dead-in-2026/)
- AI shopping agents are said to favour stores with genuine brand signals, reviews and unique product data (vendor claim). — [LetsTalkShop](https://www.letstalkshop.com/blog/is-dropshipping-dead-in-2026)
- Timeline expectation even from pro-dropshipping sources: a real business requires learning marketing, building a brand and committing for 6-12 months. — [FluentCart](https://fluentcart.com/blog/is-dropshipping-worth-it/)
- Cost pressure: Meta CPM up about 20% YoY (Triple Whale 2025) and TikTok CPM up 16% (2025). — [Triple Whale](https://triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks); [Influee](https://influee.co/blog/tiktok-ads-benchmarks)
- Regulatory pressure in the EU: the €3 duty from July 2026 with full duties expected from 2028; Consumentenbond calls for a ban on misleading dropshipping (2025, repeated Aug 2026); BEUC/Test-Aankoop actions against Temu. — [Retail Gazette](https://www.retailgazette.co.uk/blog/2026/07/shein-and-temu-hit-by-new-eu-customs-duty-on-cheap-parcels/); [Twinkle](https://twinklemagazine.nl/2026/08/consumentenbond-wil-verbod-op-misleidende-dropshipping/index.xml); [BEUC](https://www.beuc.eu/enforcement/taming-temu)

### Inferences
- The "not dead" camp is dominated by people selling dropshipping tools, supplier access or courses (Spocket, Dropship Lifestyle, Branvas, SourcingXpro, Dropship.io). Their "not dead" verdicts nearly always redefine dropshipping as branded e-commerce with outsourced fulfilment, which is a different and more capital-intensive business than the €0 generic store pitched on social media.
- For a Belgian with €0, the models that fit best are those where (a) the customer pays before you pay anything and (b) traffic does not depend on paid ads:
  - POD (Gelato, EU production) with a niche audience the person already has access to
